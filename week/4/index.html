Teach

▶ Open Chalkie slides (opens in new tab)

  1. Say: "Collaborate means we share ideas and work as a team to finish a job." Children repeat collaborate.
  2. Read the challenge: "Build a tower as tall as your tallest group member" (or wide, or mixed materials).
  3. Model planning with two volunteers: "Let's talk first. What will we use? Who goes first?"
  4. Give 1 minute of planning talk with no touching blocks.
10min

Activity

Groups build. Circulate with the Citizenship checklist and ask: "How did you decide? What did you do when you did not agree? How did you help each other?" Give a 2-minute warning.

Exit question: “I can plan with my group.” Ask 3 or 4 children to show or tell.

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Citizenship: Understanding local, national, and global issues empowers individual and collective action toward an inclusive society.

Guiding Question: How can people work together in groups?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 5): Children relate cooperation and collaboration to achieving common goals.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: People in groups cooperate and collaborate in many ways, for example, helping others, interacting fairly and respectfully, participating in activities, considering the needs of others, sharing ideas and stories, solving problems. Understanding: People can work more effectively in groups when members cooperate. Skills & Procedures: Evaluate the benefits of cooperation and collaboration.; Demonstrate ways that people can cooperate.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to relate cooperation and collaboration to reaching a common goal.
  • I can plan with my group.
  • I can help my group reach our goal.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Shares an idea, takes turns, and says one way cooperation helped the group reach its goal.
  • Evidence type: Observation (cooperation) and conversation; single-point rubric.

Materials and prep

  • Blocks, cups, or Lego for 5 groups
  • Collaboration Station challenge cards
  • Timer
  • Collaboration Station cards (PDF)
  • Citizenship conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • Cooperation single-point rubric (PDF)
  • Prep (10–15 min before class): print or open the files above, set up the room, and open this page on the classroom screen.

Room or space setup

Five building spots on the floor, one bin each, well spaced. Groups of 4 posted on the board.

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Give a clear job card (builder, holder, checker).
  • Extension: Ask the group to plan a new rule for next time.
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (collaborate, goal).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Observation (cooperation) and conversation; single-point rubric. Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Not Yet Meeting ExpectationsMeeting ExpectationsMastering Expectations
Builds alone or needs adult help to join in.Shares an idea, takes turns, and says one way cooperation helped the group reach its goal.Helps solve a disagreement and explains why cooperation made the goal easier.

Teacher records evidence in PowerTeacher Pro. A sub makes checklist notes only and leaves them for the teacher. No percentages.

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.

Sub notes

  • Materials and printables are in the Social Studies bin and linked on this page. Class list, attendance, and medical alert list are in the sub binder.
  • Ask the educational assistant or the office for help.
  • Leave for the teacher: attendance taken, checklist notes, work collected, and a short note on what was covered and any incidents.
  • Plan B (no screen or space): No blocks: groups make a shared drawing of a playground on one large sheet.
