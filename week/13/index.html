Needs and Wants · Lesson 13 of 18 · L.O. 3

What Do We Need?

Grade / Subject: Kindergarten Social Studies · Unit: Needs and Wants, lesson 1 of 2 · Time: 30 minutes · Location: classroom

▶ Open Chalkie slides (opens in new tab)Unit printables
30-minute block
05 : 00
Warm-up
Today’s focus. Needs are what we must have to live and be healthy: food, water, shelter, safety, and belonging. Some people have unique needs, like glasses or medicine.
Key words: needs

Materials

  • Teacher slide deck slide 2 (tent and hiker) or a drawing
  • Chart paper: "What We Need"
  • A pair of glasses (any)
  • L.O. 3 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • L.O. 3 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)
5min

Warm-up

Eyes closed: "Imagine we are going camping far from stores and houses. What must we have to stay alive and healthy?" Take answers and record them.

10min

Teach

▶ Open Chalkie slides (opens in new tab)

  1. Say: "A need is something we must have to live and be healthy." Children repeat needs.
  2. Circle the needs on the chart: food, water, shelter, clothing.
  3. Add: "We also need to feel safe and to belong." Link back to our class group.
  4. Show glasses. Ask: "Does everyone need glasses? Some people do. People can have unique needs."
10min

Activity

Needs walk: name an item (water, a toy car, a coat, a bed, candy). If it is a need, children stand and stretch tall; if not, they crouch. After each, ask one child: "Why?" Record on the checklist.

Printables: L.O. 3 conversation and observation checklist (PDF); L.O. 3 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)

5min

Close

Ask: "What are three things everyone needs?" Count on fingers together.

Exit question: “I can name things people need.” Ask 3 or 4 children to show or tell.

Story and video

Optional extra: a picture book to borrow and a short video. Preview before showing.

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Systems: Evaluating processes and structures of organizations builds understanding of decision making in the world.

Guiding Question: How are needs and wants different?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 3): Children explore needs and wants.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: People have basic physical and social needs.; Physical needs, including food, water, and shelter, are necessary for survival.; Social needs, including safety and belonging, relate to comfort and well-being.; Due to individual differences, people can have unique needs, for example, they may need eyeglasses, medicine, or service animals. Understanding: People can have needs and wants. Skills & Procedures: Differentiate between needs and wants.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to explore the needs people have.
  • I can name things people need.
  • I can tell why we need them.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Names basic needs and explains one reason.
  • Evidence type: Observation and conversation.

Materials and prep

  • Teacher slide deck slide 2 (tent and hiker) or a drawing
  • Chart paper: "What We Need"
  • A pair of glasses (any)
  • L.O. 3 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • L.O. 3 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)
  • Prep (10–15 min before class): print or open the files above, set up the room, and open this page on the classroom screen.

Carpet. Chart on easel.

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Picture cards.
  • Extension: Ask: "How does your family get food?"
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (needs).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Observation and conversation. Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Not Yet Meeting ExpectationsMeeting ExpectationsMastering Expectations
Mixes needs and wants with no reason.Names basic needs and explains one reason.Names physical and social needs and gives an example of a unique need.

Teacher records evidence in PowerTeacher Pro. A sub makes checklist notes only and leaves them for the teacher. No percentages.

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.

Sub notes

  • Materials and printables are in the Social Studies bin and linked on this page. Class list, attendance, and medical alert list are in the sub binder.
  • Ask the educational assistant or the office for help.

  • Plan B (no screen or space): Play the needs game seated with thumbs.
