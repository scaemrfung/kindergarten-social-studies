Places in Our Community · Lesson 6 of 18 · L.O. 1

Symbols Help Us Find Places: Where Do We Go?

Grade / Subject: Kindergarten Social Studies · Unit: Places in Our Community, lesson 2 of 3 · Time: 30 minutes · Location: classroom

Unit printables
30-minute block
05 : 00
Warm-up
Today’s focus. Places in a community have symbols. Symbols help us know a place quickly.
Key words: symbol

Materials

  • Symbol pictures: hospital +, mailbox, library book, bus stop, school crossing, Canada flag (teacher slide deck slides 7-12 or drawn)
  • 2 signs: A and B for the room sides
  • L.O. 1 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • L.O. 1 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)
5min

Warm-up

Draw a maple leaf. Ask: "What does this make you think of?" Say: "A symbol is a picture that stands for something. The maple leaf is a symbol of Canada."

10min

Teach

  1. Show each symbol one at a time. Ask: "Have you seen this? What place does it tell us about?"
  2. Link each symbol to a place on last lesson's anchor chart.
  3. Model: "I see a red cross. That means hospital. People go there when they are hurt."
  4. Explain the game: two pictures, one question, walk to the side that answers it.
10min

Activity

Where Do We Go? Ask: "Where would you go if you need groceries?" "to mail a card?" "if you are sick?" "to borrow a book?" Children walk (no running) to side A or B. Ask one child on the correct side: "How did you know?" "Who is this place important to?"

Printables: L.O. 1 conversation and observation checklist (PDF); L.O. 1 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)

5min

Close

Show one symbol. Children whisper the place to a partner, then say it together. Ask: "Why do places have symbols?"

Exit question: “I can tell what a symbol means.” Ask 3 or 4 children to show or tell.

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Time and Place: Exploring the dynamic relationships between people, place, and time supports understanding of perspectives and events to make meaning of the world.

Guiding Question: What are places within a community?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 1): Children examine places in communities.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: Places in a community can be recognized by distinctive features and symbols. Understanding: Places in communities have distinct symbols. Skills & Procedures: Recognize familiar places in the local community.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to recognize places in communities by their symbols.
  • I can tell what a symbol means.
  • I can find the place I need by its symbol.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Matches a symbol to a place and says how they knew.
  • Evidence type: Observation (movement choices) and conversation.

Materials and prep

  • Symbol pictures: hospital +, mailbox, library book, bus stop, school crossing, Canada flag (teacher slide deck slides 7-12 or drawn)
  • 2 signs: A and B for the room sides
  • L.O. 1 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • L.O. 1 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)
  • Prep (10–15 min before class): print or open the files above, set up the room, and open this page on the classroom screen.

Room or space setup

Clear the middle of the room. Tape A on one wall, B on the other.

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Walk with a partner; point instead of speak.
  • Extension: Draw a symbol for a place not shown.
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (symbol).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Observation (movement choices) and conversation. Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Not Yet Meeting ExpectationsMeeting ExpectationsMastering Expectations

Teacher records evidence in PowerTeacher Pro. A sub makes checklist notes only and leaves them for the teacher. No percentages.

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.

Sub notes

  • Materials and printables are in the Social Studies bin and linked on this page. Class list, attendance, and medical alert list are in the sub binder.
