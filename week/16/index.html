Leaders and Helpers · Lesson 16 of 18 · L.O. 4

Fair Leaders

Grade / Subject: Kindergarten Social Studies · Unit: Leaders and Helpers, lesson 2 of 2 · Time: 30 minutes · Location: classroom

▶ Open Chalkie slides (opens in new tab)Unit printables
30-minute block
05 : 00
Warm-up
Today’s focus. Being fair means sharing, taking turns, listening, and including others. Good leaders act with fairness.
Key words: fair, leader

Materials

  • Two hula hoops labelled "Fair Leader" and "Not Fair Yet"
  • Thank a Leader card, 1 per child
  • Crayons
  • Thank a Leader card (PDF)
  • Super Leader single-point rubric (PDF)
  • L.O. 4 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
5min

Warm-up

Ask: "Have you ever waited your turn? Have you ever seen someone not share?" Take two stories (no names of classmates).

10min

Teach

▶ Open Chalkie slides (opens in new tab)

  1. Say: "We can be fair by taking turns and following the rules." Choral read it twice.
  2. Say: "Fairness means sharing, taking turns, listening, and including others."
  3. Model one scenario: "A coach lets only one child play every game. Fair or not fair yet?"
  4. Explain the hoop game.
10min

Activity

Read 5 scenarios (teacher gives everyone a turn; principal listens to both sides; a captain picks only friends; nurse helps the sickest first; firefighter shares safety tips with everyone). Children step to a hoop. Ask: "Why?" Then children colour a Thank a Leader card for a fair leader.

Printables: Thank a Leader card (PDF); Super Leader single-point rubric (PDF); L.O. 4 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)

5min

Close

Ask: "How can you be a fair leader at recess?" Two answers. Collect cards to deliver.

Exit question: “I can tell if a leader is being fair.” Ask 3 or 4 children to show or tell.

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Systems: Evaluating processes and structures of organizations builds understanding of decision making in the world.

Guiding Question: In what ways might leaders be important to communities?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 4): Children investigate the role of leaders in communities.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: Leaders can contribute to the well-being of a community by acting with fairness. Understanding: Leaders guide communities. Skills & Procedures: Recognize ways leaders guide communities.; Brainstorm ways leaders can act with fairness.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to brainstorm ways leaders can act with fairness.
  • I can tell if a leader is being fair.
  • I can show a fair way to lead.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Judges a scenario as fair or not fair and gives a reason.
  • Evidence type: Observation and conversation; Super Leader single-point rubric.

Materials and prep

  • Two hula hoops labelled "Fair Leader" and "Not Fair Yet"
  • Thank a Leader card, 1 per child
  • Crayons
  • Thank a Leader card (PDF)
  • Super Leader single-point rubric (PDF)
  • L.O. 4 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • Prep (10–15 min before class): print or open the files above, set up the room, and open this page on the classroom screen.

Room or space setup

Hoops on the floor with space around them.

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Thumbs up / down from the carpet.
  • Extension: Write or dictate a thank-you sentence.
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (fair, leader).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Observation and conversation; Super Leader single-point rubric. Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Not Yet Meeting ExpectationsMeeting ExpectationsMastering Expectations
Chooses a hoop without a reason.Judges a scenario as fair or not fair and gives a reason.Suggests how to change an unfair action into a fair one.

Teacher records evidence in PowerTeacher Pro. A sub makes checklist notes only and leaves them for the teacher. No percentages.

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.

Sub notes

  • Materials and printables are in the Social Studies bin and linked on this page. Class list, attendance, and medical alert list are in the sub binder.
  • Ask the educational assistant or the office for help.

  • Plan B (no screen or space): Seated version with thumbs.
