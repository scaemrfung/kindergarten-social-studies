Long Ago and Now: Family Histories

  1. Say: "History is what happened in the past." Children repeat history.
  2. Read part of When I Was Little or tell a short story about yourself long ago and now.
  3. Ask: "What happened in the past in this story? What is happening now?"

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Time and Place: Exploring the dynamic relationships between people, place, and time supports understanding of perspectives and events to make meaning of the world.

Guiding Question: How can sharing cultures and histories build connections between people?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 2): Children explore expressions of traditions, cultures, and histories.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: Histories are records of events that happened in the past (family history, personal history).; Events can be described according to sequence in time, for example, recently, long ago, daily, yearly. Understanding: Traditions, cultures, and histories can build understanding of self and others. Skills & Procedures: Share experiences with reference to time.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to share experiences with reference to time.
  • I can tell something from long ago.
  • I can tell something from now.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Describes a personal or family event using time words (long ago, now, every year).
  • Evidence type: Conversation and product (quilt square); Traditions rubric.

Materials and prep

  • History bag: cloth bag with a baby rattle, an old toy, a photo of a birthday cake, a shoe
  • Family History Quilt square, 1 per child
  • Optional: When I Was Little by Jamie Lee Curtis
  • Zoom In prompt (PDF)
  • My Family History Quilt square (PDF)

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Use the item from the bag as a prompt.
  • Extension: Interview a family member at home (questionnaire).
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (history, long ago, now).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Conversation and product (quilt square); Traditions rubric. Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.
