We Belong to Groups

Grade / Subject: Kindergarten Social Studies · Unit: Working Together, lesson 1 of 4 · Time: 30 minutes · Location: classroom

30-minute block

Warm-up
Today’s focus. A group is people who do something together. Each of us belongs to many groups: family, class, team, church, friends.
Key words: group, belong

  • Chart paper: "Groups I Belong To"

Warm-up

Show one circle you coloured to stand for you. Say: "This circle is me. Right now it is floating around. It does not know where it belongs!" Say "belong". Students repeat it and clap the two parts: be-long.

10min

Teach: Big Book read-aloud

Read the Big Book on the screen with the viewer below. Use ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys. Full screen works on the SMART Board.

  1. Read the Big Book My Groups. Pause on each page. Ask: "Who is in this group? What do they do together?"
  2. Write group on the chart. Say: "A group is people who do something together."
  3. Place your circle in the hoop. Say: "My class is a group. I belong here." Add 2 more groups you belong to (family, choir).
  4. Turn and talk: "Tell a partner one group you belong to." Record 4 or 5 answers on the chart with a quick sketch.

10min

Activity

At tables, children colour three circles to show themselves (for example, favourite colour). On each circle you scribe or they draw one group: family, class, and one more. Circulate with the checklist. Ask: "What does this group do together?"

5min

Close

Back on the carpet, three children put a circle in the hoop and name the group. Ask: "Can one person belong to many groups?" Thumbs up or down. Say: "We all belong to our class group."

Exit question: “I can name a group I belong to.” Ask 3 or 4 children to show or tell.

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Citizenship: Understanding local, national, and global issues empowers individual and collective action toward an inclusive society.

Guiding Question: How can people work together in groups?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 5): Children relate cooperation and collaboration to achieving common goals.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: People in groups cooperate and collaborate in many ways, for example, helping others, interacting fairly and respectfully, participating in activities, considering the needs of others, sharing ideas and stories, solving problems. Understanding: People can work more effectively in groups when members cooperate. Skills & Procedures: Identify groups of personal belonging.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to identify groups we belong to and why groups matter.
  • I can name a group I belong to.
  • I can tell what my group does together.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Names at least one group of personal belonging and says what the group does.
  • Evidence type: Conversation and product (coloured group circles).

Materials and prep

  • Big Book: My Groups (page viewer on this page)
  • 1 set of My Groups circles per child, pre-cut (prep 15 min)
  • Crayons; 1 hula hoop or yarn circle

Carpet for the read. One hoop in the middle of the carpet. Tables set with crayons and circles.

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Offer picture cards of groups (family, class, team). Accept pointing.
  • Extension: Ask: "How do you help your group?"
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (group, belong).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Conversation and product (coloured group circles). Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Not Yet Meeting ExpectationsMeeting ExpectationsMastering Expectations

Teacher records evidence in PowerTeacher Pro. A sub makes checklist notes only and leaves them for the teacher. No percentages.

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.

Sub notes

  • Materials and printables are in the Social Studies bin and linked on this page. Class list, attendance, and medical alert list are in the sub binder.
  • Ask the educational assistant or the office for help.
  • Leave for the teacher: attendance taken, checklist notes, work collected, and a short note on what was covered and any incidents.
  • Plan B (no screen or space): Read the Big Book aloud from the printed copy or tell it from memory; sort children into groups by shoe type and talk about belonging.
