Who Helps? Leaders in Our Community

Grade / Subject: Kindergarten Social Studies · Unit: Leaders and Helpers, lesson 1 of 2 · Time: 30 minutes · Location: classroom

Unit printables
30-minute block
05 : 00
Warm-up
Today’s focus. A leader helps a group reach a goal or figure out what to do. There are leaders at home, at school, and in the community.
Key words: leader, helper

Materials

  • Big Book: Who Helps?
  • Community leader figure cards (from the toolkit) or drawn pictures: teacher, principal, coach, nurse, firefighter, pastor, Elder
  • L.O. 4 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • L.O. 4 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)
5min

Warm-up

Follow the Leader: you do simple actions (tap nose, march). Switch leaders three times. Ask: "How did you know what to do?"

10min

Teach: Big Book read-aloud

Read the Big Book on the screen with the viewer below. Use ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys. Full screen works on the SMART Board.

  1. Say: "A leader is someone who helps a group reach a goal or figure out what to do." Children repeat leader.
  2. Read Who Helps?. On each page ask: "Who is helping? What can I do to help?"
  3. Show leader cards. Ask: "Where does this leader help: home, school, or community?"
  4. Model: "A principal is a leader. She helps our school stay safe and learn."

Big Book 11: Who Helps?

10min

Activity

Sort leader cards into three hoops: home, school, community. Each child places one card and says: "A ___ is a leader. They help by ___." Record on the checklist.

5min

Close

Ask: "Who is a leader in your life?" Whisper to a partner. Say: "You can be a helper and a leader too."

Exit question: “I can name a leader.” Ask 3 or 4 children to show or tell.

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Systems: Evaluating processes and structures of organizations builds understanding of decision making in the world.

Guiding Question: In what ways might leaders be important to communities?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 4): Children investigate the role of leaders in communities.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: Leaders guide group members and help communities achieve goals.; There are many leadership roles in communities such as schools and families, for example, teachers, principals, parents, Elders, coaches. Understanding: Leaders guide communities. Skills & Procedures: Identify leaders in the local community.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to identify leaders in our local community.
  • I can name a leader.
  • I can tell how a leader helps.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Identifies a community leader and how they help.
  • Evidence type: Conversation and observation.

Materials and prep

  • Big Book: Who Helps?
  • Community leader figure cards (from the toolkit) or drawn pictures: teacher, principal, coach, nurse, firefighter, pastor, Elder
  • L.O. 4 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • L.O. 4 all-in-one resource collection (PDF)
  • Prep (10–15 min before class): print or open the files above, set up the room, and open this page on the classroom screen.

Room or space setup

Clear space for Follow the Leader, then carpet.

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Pair with a partner to place cards.
  • Extension: Ask: "What tools does this leader use?"
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (leader, helper).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Conversation and observation. Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.

Sub notes
