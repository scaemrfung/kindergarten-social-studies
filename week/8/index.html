Traditions We Celebrate

Grade / Subject: Kindergarten Social Studies · Unit: Me, My Family, My Traditions, lesson 1 of 5 · Time: 30 minutes · Location: classroom

Today’s focus. Traditions are special things families and groups do again and again, like birthdays, Christmas, and family meals.
Key words: tradition, celebrate

5min

Warm-up

Ask: "What do you do on your birthday?" Take three answers. Say: "Many families do that every year. That is a tradition."

10min

Teach

▶ Open Chalkie slides (opens in new tab)

  1. Say: "A tradition is something special we do again and again." Children repeat tradition.
  2. Note: families have different traditions. Invite all to share; do not assume.
  3. Brainstorm a class list: birthdays, Christmas, Thanksgiving dinner, Lunar New Year, Diwali, Sunday dinner.
  4. Ask about each: "How often? Every day, every week, or every year? Who is there?"

Faith connection · optional · Luke 2:10-11

“I bring you good news that will cause great joy for all the people. Today in the town of David a Saviour has been born to you.” (NIV)

Christmas is a tradition for many families at SCA. Optional, not Alberta curriculum. Keep the activity open to every family's traditions.

10min

Activity

Move to the card: call a tradition ("birthday candles"). Children walk, hop, or tiptoe to the matching card. Say: "Families celebrate birthdays all over the world. Every year!" Then children draw a family tradition on the exit ticket.

Printables: See, Think, Wonder prompt (PDF); My Tradition exit ticket (PDF); L.O. 2 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)

5min

Close

Collect exit tickets. Ask: "Does every family have the same traditions? Why is it good to learn about others' traditions?"

Exit question: “I can tell about a tradition in my family.” Ask 3 or 4 children to show or tell.

Full lesson plan (teacher and sub)

Same headings and order as the Alberta lesson plan standard. A substitute can teach from this page alone.

Curriculum outcomes

Social Studies K–6 (2025), Kindergarten

Organizing Idea: Time and Place: Exploring the dynamic relationships between people, place, and time supports understanding of perspectives and events to make meaning of the world.

Guiding Question: How can sharing cultures and histories build connections between people?

Learning Outcome (L.O. 2): Children explore expressions of traditions, cultures, and histories.

KUSP focus for this lesson: Knowledge: Traditions are beliefs and behaviours that are passed down through generations, for example, celebrations, ceremonies, holidays, foods, music, activities, stories. Understanding: Traditions, cultures, and histories can build understanding of self and others. Skills & Procedures: Share traditions, cultures, languages, and histories of personal or family significance.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to explore traditions of personal and family significance.
  • I can tell about a tradition in my family.
  • I can say how often we do it.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Draws and names a tradition and tells when it happens (every year, every week).
  • Evidence type: Product (exit ticket) and conversation.

Materials and prep

  • Tradition picture cards (birthday cake, Christmas tree, fireworks, family meal, bedtime story) printed from the toolkit or drawn
  • My Tradition exit ticket, 1 per child
  • Crayons
  • See, Think, Wonder prompt (PDF)
  • My Tradition exit ticket (PDF)
  • L.O. 2 conversation and observation checklist (PDF)
  • Prep (10–15 min before class): print or open the files above, set up the room, and open this page on the classroom screen.

Room or space setup

Tape the five picture cards around the room at child height.

Safety notes

  • Movement is walking pace only, in the cleared space, away from tables and walls.
  • Stop signal: say “Freeze, hands on head.” Children stop, look at you, and listen.
  • Scissors and small items: model safe use; collect before the close.
  • Check the class medical alert list in the sub binder.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

  • Support: Point to a picture card to start the drawing.
  • Extension: Add who is there and what you eat.
  • ISP/accommodations: Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Language learners: show pictures, model first, and post the key words (tradition, celebrate).

Classroom management cues

  • Attention signal: “Hands on top!” Children answer “That means stop!”
  • Transitions: count down from 5 to move from carpet to tables.
  • Groups: use the posted groups or carpet rows. Hand out materials by table helpers.
  • A child who needs a break may use the calm corner, then rejoin.

Assessment

Tool: Product (exit ticket) and conversation. Use the checklist from the printables. EIPS K–2 levels:

Exit and clean-up

  • Table helpers collect materials. Put papers in the class folder.
  • Return to the carpet or line up for the next class.

Sub notes

  • Materials and printables are in the Social Studies bin and linked on this page. Class list, attendance, and medical alert list are in the sub binder.

  • Plan B (no screen or space): Play the movement game with children pointing from the carpet.
